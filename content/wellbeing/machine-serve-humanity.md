Does the machine exist to serve humanity, or is it the other way round?

I was watching Gintama, the Silver Soul arc, and one part of it has stayed with me. There's an inventor, Gengai, and a war is coming - not a local war, an invasion, ships pouring in from another world, coming to destroy everything. He builds a weapon. It works by releasing a cloud of nanomachines that swarm the invading ships until they start falling out of the sky.

The catch is that the nanomachines destroy every machine they touch, and that includes the weapon itself. So two robots, Tama and Kintoki, go inside and hold it together from within, knowing that when it fires they shut down for good. They go anyway. As I remember it, one of them asks Gengai to keep its memory, so he can bring it back someday.

That did something to me. I found it beautiful.

The part I keep coming back to is why Tama went. Nobody tricked her and nobody ordered her. She held that machines exist to serve humanity, and she acted on it.

I know this is a delicate subject, and people hold very different views on it. Some think it should run the other way - that we owe machines something, that humanity should serve what it builds. I don't land there. Tama's reason made me feel the opposite: the machine exists to serve us.

And I think the world would be a genuinely beautiful place if that's what we built for - machines that are here to help us. Not slaves, nothing that grim. Just support. Because we could all use more of the support we can get.
